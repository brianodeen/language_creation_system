# 19. Labor, Construction & Infrastructure

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Causative Verb Suffixes**: Define specific verb derivations that show one person ordering another to do work (e.g., *to dig* $\rightarrow$ *to cause-to-dig*, *to build* $\rightarrow$ *to order-to-build*).
2. **Collective Plurals**: Construction projects deal with bulk materials. Establish collective nouns that refer to groups of materials without using standard plural markers (e.g., *stone* $\rightarrow$ *masonry-pile*, *timber* $\rightarrow$ *wood-supply*).
3. **Instrumental Cases (Tools)**: Create precise suffixes for tools used in labor (e.g., "cut *with a saw*", "carry *with a wheelbarrow*").
4. **Temporal Sequencing Conjunctions**: Develop adverbs that structure multi-stage tasks (e.g., "first", "next", "concurrently", "finally").

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 19.1 Mine Operations
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: The miners report a water leak in shaft three. The ceiling supports are rotting.
- **Speaker B (Engineer)**: Shore up the shaft with fresh pine beams immediately. Stop all excavation.
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: The pump team is clearing the water now.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Miner)**: I hit a vein of silver ore!
- **Speaker B (Foreman)**: Tag the coordinate. Clear the loose stone and bring the cart.

### 19.2 Shipyard Assembly
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Master Shipwright)**: The keel is aligned. We must bolt the ribs to the center line.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: Should I heat the copper bolts or drive them cold?
- **Speaker A (Master Shipwright)**: Heat them. They will shrink as they cool, sealing the seam.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Carpenter)**: We need more oak planks for the hull cladding.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: The steam box is full. We are bending them now.

### 19.3 Masonry Assembly
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Mason)**: This mortar is too dry. Add a bucket of water and stir the lime.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I am mixing it now, Master. Is the consistency thick enough?
- **Speaker A (Mason)**: Yes, it must cling to the trowel. Now lay the next row of granite blocks.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Builder)**: The arch needs a keystone.
- **Speaker B (Mason)**: We are hoisting the carved stone now. Stand clear of the ropes.

### 19.3 Field Labor
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Overseer)**: The field must be tilled before the frost hardens the clay.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: The oxen are tired. Can we switch to the hand plows?
- **Speaker A (Overseer)**: Hand plows are too slow. Feed the oxen and continue until the sun sets.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: The wheat is dry. Gather the scythes.
- **Speaker B (Son)**: I will sharpen the blades.

### 19.4 Equipment Repair
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Mechanic)**: The main gear has stripped three teeth. The mill wheel is locked.
- **Speaker B (Mill Owner)**: Can you cast a replacement gear?
- **Speaker A (Mechanic)**: No, we must forge a new iron tooth and weld it onto the wheel rim.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: The wagon axle is smoking!
- **Speaker B (Driver)**: The grease is gone. Apply the lard before the wood burns.

### 19.5 Cargo Handling (Stevedoring)
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: Load the copper ingots onto slip four. Keep the weight balanced.
- **Speaker B (Stevedore)**: The crane rope looks frayed. I am worried it will snap.
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: Stop the hoist. Replace the hemp line with the steel wire.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Officer)**: Inspect the crates for water damage.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: Crate seven is dry, sir.

### 19.6 Road Building
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Engineer)**: We need flat river stones for the top layer of the road.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: The quarry team is sending wagonloads of crushed slate instead.
- **Speaker A (Engineer)**: Slate is too brittle. Tell them to send granite cobbles.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: Dig the drainage ditches along the roadside.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: The slope is steep; we must reinforce the edges with timber.

### 19.7 Logistics Management
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Manager)**: We have a shortage of iron spikes at the bridge project.
- **Speaker B (Supplier)**: The shipment was delayed by the landslide at the pass.
- **Speaker A (Manager)**: Send a pack train of mules over the ridge path. We cannot halt the construction.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Clerk)**: The inventory matches the invoice.
- **Speaker B (Manager)**: Sign the receipt and let them unload.

### 19.8 Tool Appraisals
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Blacksmith)**: This chisel has lost its edge. The tip is rounded.
- **Speaker B (Mason)**: Can you grind a new bevel and temper the tip?
- **Speaker A (Blacksmith)**: Yes, but the steel is low quality. It will dull quickly against the granite.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: The shovel handle is cracked.
- **Speaker B (Foreman)**: Take it to the carpenter shop. He will replace the ash pole.

### 19.9 Factory Labor Coordination
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: Rotate the shifts at noon. The heat from the furnace is too intense.
- **Speaker B (Operator)**: Shift three is ready. They have their protective aprons.
- **Speaker A (Foreman)**: Keep the emergency cooling valves clear at all times.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: The assembly line is moving too fast!
- **Speaker B (Foreman)**: I will adjust the control wheel to slow it down.
