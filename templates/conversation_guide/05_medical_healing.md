# 5. Medical, Healing & Medicine

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Patientive/Stative Alignment (Bodily States)**: Many languages express bodily feelings using passive or indirect constructions (e.g., "headache affects me" or "I am sickened" rather than active "I have a headache").
2. **Involuntary Agent Syntax**: Define how to mark actions that happen without the speaker's intent (e.g., "I coughed accidentally", "my bone broke").
3. **Accusative of Body Parts**: Establish syntax for actions targetting body parts (e.g., "bind *my arm*", "clean *the wound*").
4. **Intensive/Attenuative Aspect**: Create verb prefixes or suffixes that indicate severity of pain or healing progress (e.g., *pain-intense* vs. *pain-diminishing*).

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 5.1 Clinic Intake
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Clerk)**: State your name and age for the register.
- **Speaker B (Patient)**: I am Elena, age thirty-four.
- **Speaker A (Clerk)**: What brings you to the clinic today?
- **Speaker B (Patient)**: I have a persistent cough and a burning fever that started two nights ago.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Healer)**: Have you visited this sanctuary before?
- **Speaker B (Patient)**: No, Healer. This is my first time.

### 5.2 Diagnosing Illness
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Healer)**: Let me feel your pulse. Yes, it is rapid and weak. Open your mouth.
- **Speaker B (Patient)**: Does it look serious, Healer?
- **Speaker A (Healer)**: Your throat is inflamed. You have the swamp-ague. I will write a prescription for willow-bark tea.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Patient)**: My side hurts when I breathe deeply.
- **Speaker B (Healer)**: Lie down. Let me press here. Tell me if the pain is sharp.

### 5.3 Apothecary Orders
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Apothecary)**: How can I help you, traveler?
- **Speaker B (Customer)**: I need dried lavender, peppermint oil, and a poultice for a sprain.
- **Speaker A (Apothecary)**: Here is the oil, and the lavender is fresh. The poultice should be applied twice daily.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Customer)**: Do you have anything to soothe a child's stomach ache?
- **Speaker B (Apothecary)**: Ginger syrup is best. Five drops in warm water.

### 5.4 First Aid & Stitches
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Rescuer)**: Hold this bandage tight against the cut. We need to stop the bleeding.
- **Speaker B (Wounded)**: Ah! It burns!
- **Speaker A (Rescuer)**: Keep pressure on it. I need to thread the needle to sew this closed.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Medic)**: The arm is broken. I must set the bone straight.
- **Speaker B (Patient)**: Tell me when you are ready. I will bite on this leather strap.

### 5.5 Surgical Procedures
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Surgeon)**: Boil the water and sterilize the knives. We must remove the arrow head.
- **Speaker B (Assistant)**: The patient is asleep from the poppy juice.
- **Speaker A (Surgeon)**: Good. Hold the leg steady. I am making the incision now.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Surgeon)**: The wound is infected. We must burn it clean.
- **Speaker B (Assistant)**: The iron is hot. Ready on your mark.

- **Speaker B (Supplicant)**: I feel a warmth spreading through my chest.

### 5.7 Poisoning & Antidotes
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Companion)**: He ate the blue berries! His lips are swelling.
- **Speaker B (Healer)**: Quick, make him drink salt-water to purge his stomach.
- **Speaker A (Companion)**: I have the charcoal paste ready. Should I administer it?
- **Speaker B (Healer)**: Yes, immediately after he vomits.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Patient)**: I was bitten by a sand-viper!
- **Speaker B (Medic)**: Keep the limb low. Do not walk. I will apply the anti-venom root extract.

### 5.8 Medical Triage
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Medic 1)**: We have twenty casualties from the mining collapse.
- **Speaker B (Medic 2)**: Separate them. Treat those with head trauma and arterial bleeding first.
- **Speaker A (Medic 1)**: What about those with broken ribs?
- **Speaker B (Medic 2)**: Bandage them and place them in the second tent. They can wait.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Responder)**: Is he breathing?
- **Speaker B (Medic)**: Yes, but his pulse is faint. Place him on the left side to keep his airway clear.

### 5.9 Epidemics & Quarantines
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Guard)**: The village is under quarantine. No one may enter or leave by order of the magistrate.
- **Speaker B (Traveler)**: I only need to pass through to reach the port.
- **Speaker A (Guard)**: Detour around the valley. The red plague is spreading fast.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Healer)**: All citizens must boil their drinking water. The well is contaminated.
- **Speaker B (Citizen)**: I will spread the warning to the southern quarter.

### 5.10 Convalescence & Recovery
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Healer)**: You must remain in bed for another week. The fever is gone, but your heart is weak.
- **Speaker B (Patient)**: I feel restless, Healer. I have work to do.
- **Speaker A (Healer)**: If you exert yourself, the sickness will return. Drink your broth and sleep.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Patient)**: Look, I can move my fingers today.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Praise the spirits! The healing takes time, but you are recovering.
