# 20. Time, Calendar & Seasons

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Tense Systems**: Establish the primary temporal markers (e.g., past, present, future). Decide if the language uses a binary tense system (future vs. non-future) or a multi-tiered past system (recent past, remote past, mythological past).
2. **Aspect Systems**: Define the grammatical distinction between completed actions (**Perfective Aspect**) and ongoing/habitual actions (**Imperfective Aspect**).
3. **Temporal Adverbs**: Create roots for relative time references (e.g., "today", "yesterday", "tomorrow", "formerly", "henceforth").
4. **Durative vs. Punctual Markers**: Differentiate actions that happen instantly (e.g., "the bell rang") from actions that take duration (e.g., "she slept all night").

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 20.1 Scheduling Meetings
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Diplomat)**: We must schedule a meeting to sign the treaty.
- **Speaker B (Secretary)**: The Council is free on the third day after the new moon, in the morning.
- **Speaker A (Diplomat)**: That is acceptable. We will gather at the third bell in the main hall.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Friend)**: Let's meet at the tavern tonight.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Sounds good. I'll be there after the sunset bell.

### 20.2 Celestial Cycles
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Astronomer)**: The solar eclipse will occur at noon on the winter solstice.
- **Speaker B (Priest)**: The alignment indicates the end of the second cycle. We must prepare the ritual fires.
- **Speaker A (Astronomer)**: The duration will be three minutes of absolute darkness.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Sailor)**: The moon is full tonight.
- **Speaker B (Captain)**: The tide will be high. We can clear the shallow reefs easily.

### 20.3 Historical Eras
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Scholar)**: This manuscript was written during the Reign of the Three Suns, before the great fire.
- **Speaker B (Student)**: How long did that dynasty last, Master?
- **Speaker A (Scholar)**: Approximately four hundred solar cycles. It was an era of peace.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Elder)**: I remember the years before the capital fell.
- **Speaker B (Youth)**: Was the city truly as magnificent as the ballads sing?

### 20.4 Seasonal Planning
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: The autumn wind is cold. We must dig up the root crops before the ground freezes.
- **Speaker B (Son)**: What about the grain storage? Is the roof sealed?
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: We will seal the roof tomorrow. The carrots are the priority today.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Overseer)**: The river rises in the spring. We must clear the canal gates now.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: I will assemble the shovel crew.

### 20.5 Travel Times & Durations
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: How long is the sea transit to the eastern islands?
- **Speaker B (Captain)**: With a good wind, it is a twelve-day voyage. If the doldrums catch us, it can take a month.
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: We will pack provisions for forty days, just in case.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: Can we reach the valley by sundown?
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: Yes, if we maintain a steady trot.

### 20.6 Recounting History
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Chronicler)**: "Year eighty-two. The volcanic eruption destroyed the northern watchtower. The skies remained ash-filled for three seasons."
- **Speaker B (Student)**: Did the garrison survive?
- **Speaker A (Chronicler)**: They retreated to the capital, leaving the border open to the tribes.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Elder)**: The battle of the river occurred when I was ten years old.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: Did you see the shields clashing?

### 20.7 Calibrating Clocks & Hourglasses
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Clerk)**: The sand in this hourglass is running too fast. The neck is worn.
- **Speaker B (Master)**: Replace the glass module. We need precise timing for the shift changes.
- **Speaker A (Clerk)**: I will calibrate it against the sundial at noon.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Technician)**: The clock gear is skipping.
- **Speaker B (Horologist)**: Lubricate the escapement mechanism.

### 20.8 Age & Lifespans
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Visitor)**: How old is the village elder?
- **Speaker B (Local)**: He has seen ninety winter solstices. His memory is still sharp.
- **Speaker A (Visitor)**: That is a remarkable lifespan for this region.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Child)**: I will be ten years old tomorrow!
- **Speaker B (Grandmother)**: Happy birthday, young one. You are growing fast.

### 20.9 Past Habits vs. Future Hopes
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Elder)**: We used to fish in the creek when the water was clean.
- **Speaker B (Youth)**: Now the mines have poisoned the current.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Soldier)**: Formerly, I was a weaver.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: And in the future, we will return to our looms.
