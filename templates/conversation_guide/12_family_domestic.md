# 12. Family, Kinship & Domestic Life

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Kinship Affixes**: Many languages attach specific prefixes or suffixes to indicate kinship relation and ownership (e.g., "my-mother", "thy-brother").
2. **Dual & Trial Numbers**: Family dynamics often occur in pairs or small groups. Establish dual ("two brothers") and trial ("three sisters") grammatical markers if appropriate.
3. **Possessive Suffix System**: Define how personal possession is marked directly on household items (e.g., "our-house", "your-broom").
4. **Cohortative Mood**: Create a verb inflection representing group action within the household (e.g., "Let's clean the hearth", "let's eat dinner").

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 12.1 Parenting & Instruction
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Parent)**: Put down that sharp knife, young one. You must hold it by the handle, not the blade.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: Like this, mother?
- **Speaker A (Parent)**: Yes, now slice the roots slowly. Keep your fingers tucked back.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Father)**: It is past sunset. Brush your hair and get under the blankets.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: Will you tell us a story about the mountain spirits?
- **Speaker A (Father)**: Tomorrow. Tonight, sleep.

### 12.2 Distributing Chores
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Parent)**: Children, we need to prepare the house for the visitors.
- **Speaker B (Sibling A)**: I will sweep the floors and wash the wood benches.
- **Speaker C (Sibling B)**: And I will fetch fresh water and polish the copper kettle.
- **Speaker A (Parent)**: Good. I will start the stew.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Husband)**: The sheep must be sheared today.
- **Speaker B (Wife)**: I will assist you. Let the kids gather the wool into sacks.

### 12.3 Sibling Arguments
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Sibling A)**: Give that back! It was a gift from grandfather!
- **Speaker B (Sibling B)**: You weren't using it. I just wanted to examine the carvings.
- **Speaker A (Sibling A)**: You always take my things without asking. Ask first!
- **Speaker B (Sibling B)**: Fine, here it is. Sorry.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Brother)**: You took the larger piece of bread!
- **Speaker B (Sister)**: I did the heavy lifting at the well today. I earned it.

### 12.4 Attire & Grooming
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Wife)**: Your travel cloak is torn along the seam. Let me mend it.
- **Speaker B (Husband)**: Thank you. I also need to wash these trousers; the road was mud-soaked.
- **Speaker A (Wife)**: Place them in the wash bucket. I am heating the soap-water.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Mother)**: Your hair is a nest of twigs. Sit down so I can comb it.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: Gently, mother! The knots hurt.

### 12.5 Ancestral History
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Grandmother)**: Gather close, children. This tapestry was woven by your great-grandmother when our clan lived by the southern river.
- **Speaker B (Grandchild)**: Why did we leave the river, grandmother?
- **Speaker A (Grandmother)**: The river rose and flooded our fields, so we climbed the hills to build this farm.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Youth)**: Whose crest is carved on this iron shield?
- **Speaker B (Elder)**: That is the crest of your uncle, who defended the city gates fifty years ago.

### 12.6 Family Inheritances
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Elder Brother)**: Father left the pasture to you and the house to me.
- **Speaker B (Younger Brother)**: That is fair, but what about the tools in the forge?
- **Speaker A (Elder Brother)**: We shall share them. We both need the anvil.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Daughter)**: I request mother's silver loom.
- **Speaker B (Father)**: It is yours, my daughter. Keep it in your household and teach your girls.

### 12.7 Household Rules
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Father)**: In this house, we do not lock the doors during the day. Guests are always welcome.
- **Speaker B (Guest)**: That is a generous custom. In my city, we lock everything.
- **Speaker A (Father)**: Here, trust is our primary wall.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Mother)**: No weapons are permitted at the dining table. Leave the daggers on the shelf.
- **Speaker B (Son)**: Understood, mother.

### 12.8 Family Reunions
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Aunt)**: Look how tall you have grown! I remember when you were a babe in arms.
- **Speaker B (Nephew)**: It is wonderful to see you, Aunt Sarah. Have you traveled far?
- **Speaker A (Aunt)**: Three days' ride. Your cousins are right behind me with the wagon.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Host)**: The tables are set. Let the family feast begin!
- **Speaker B (Relative)**: Pour the plum wine!

### 12.9 Marital Discussions
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Husband)**: We must decide whether to sell the young bull at the market.
- **Speaker B (Wife)**: We need the silver to buy winter coats for the children.
- **Speaker A (Husband)**: Yes, but if we keep him, we can breed him next year.
- **Speaker B (Wife)**: The kids' health cannot wait until next year. Sell him.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Wife)**: I want to expand the vegetable garden into the southern patch.
- **Speaker B (Husband)**: I will clear the stones for you this weekend.

### 12.10 Care of Elders
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Son)**: Grandfather, let me help you up the stone steps.
- **Speaker B (Elder)**: Thank you, my boy. My knees are stiff today when the damp air rolls in.
- **Speaker A (Son)**: I will stoke the hearth fire to warm your seat.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Daughter)**: Have you taken your herbal tea, mother?
- **Speaker B (Elder)**: Not yet, dear. Bring it with some honey.
