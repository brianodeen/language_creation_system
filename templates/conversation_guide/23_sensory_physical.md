# 23. Sensory Perceptions & Physical States

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Sensory Verbs**: Define distinct verbs for sensory actions (active: "I look at", "I listen to") vs. sensory perceptions (passive/stative: "it appears", "it sounds", "it smells", "it tastes").
2. **Sensation Syntax**: Outline how physical states are expressed grammatically (e.g., direct predicate: "I am cold" vs. nominal subject: "coldness affects me" vs. inchoative: "I grow cold").
3. **Spatial Coordinates**: Establish relative distance and placement markers (e.g., proximity: "right here", mid-distance: "there", remote: "yonder").
4. **Descriptive Adjective Agreement**: Ensure adjectives describing colors, textures, and temperatures agree with the noun class/gender.

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 23.1 Hunger & Thirst
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: My throat is dry. I have not tasted water since the noon bell.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: We have one cup left in the skin. Sip it slowly.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Thank you. I feel my strength returning.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Child)**: Mother, my stomach is growling. Is the bread baked?
- **Speaker B (Mother)**: Almost, child. Eat these dried apple slices in the meantime.

### 23.2 Extreme Temperatures
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: The wind is freezing. My fingers are completely numb.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Rub them together. We must stoke the hearth fire immediately.
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: I can barely hold the firewood.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: The sun is baking the field. The heat is making me dizzy.
- **Speaker B (Foreman)**: Go sit under the shadow of the willow tree and drink water.

### 23.3 Sounds in the Dark
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Sentry)**: Shh. Did you hear that? A dry twig snapped in the brush.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: I only hear the crickets.
- **Speaker A (Sentry)**: No, listen closely... there it is again. A heavy step. Raise your bow.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: What is that hollow rumbling sound?
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: The ice sheet is shifting on the lake. It is safe.

### 23.4 Scents & Smoke
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: Do you smell that? It is the scent of burning pine.
- **Speaker B (Commander)**: A campfire? Or a forest fire?
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: The smoke is thin and sweet. A campsite is nearby.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Alchemist)**: The vapors from this potion are toxic.
- **Speaker B (Assistant)**: I will open the windows to clear the air.

### 23.5 Fatigue & Sleepiness
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: My eyelids are heavy. I cannot walk another mile.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: Let us camp under this rocky ledge. We will take turns on watch.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Good. I will sleep first.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Child)**: I cannot keep my eyes open, mother.
- **Speaker B (Mother)**: Lay down, little one. The night is quiet.

### 23.6 Surface Textures
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Buyer)**: This wool is rough and scratchy. It will irritate the skin.
- **Speaker B (Merchant)**: It is raw wool, unwashed. Touch this roll of fine linen instead.
- **Speaker A (Buyer)**: Ah, yes. This is smooth and soft.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Builder)**: The stone block is too rough.
- **Speaker B (Mason)**: I will use the sand-paper to polish the surface.

### 23.7 Culinary Taste
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Guest)**: This stew is very salty, and the herbs are bitter.
- **Speaker B (Host)**: My apologies. I will add some honey and cream to soften the flavor.
- **Speaker A (Guest)**: That is much better. The bitterness is gone.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Child)**: This berry is sweet!
- **Speaker B (Parent)**: Eat only the ripe ones. The green ones are sour and will make you sick.

### 23.8 Illusions vs. Physical Reality
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Look! A lake of clear water ahead in the valley!
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: That is only a heat mirage. The ground is dry salt.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: But it looks so real! I see the reflection of the hills.
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: Do not chase the mirage. Keep to the trail.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Scholar)**: Is that figure a ghost?
- **Speaker B (Healer)**: No, it is the steam rising from the hot spring, catching the moonlight.

### 23.9 Spatial Placement
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: Where is the entrance to the tomb hidden?
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: It is high above us, behind that large ivy cluster.
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: I see the ivy. Is there a ledge to climb?
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: Yes, directly below it.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Carpenter)**: Place the beam adjacent to the pillar.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: Doing so now. Is it aligned?

### 23.10 Colors & Brightness
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Artist)**: The dye is a deep crimson, like the autumn sunset.
- **Speaker B (Weaver)**: It will look beautiful on the wool cloak. The color is very bright.
- **Speaker A (Artist)**: Yes, but keep it out of the direct sun or it will fade.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Navigator)**: The morning light is blinding.
- **Speaker B (Captain)**: Wear the brimmed hat to shade your eyes.
