# 9. Nature, Weather & Survival

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Impersonal Weather Verbs**: Define how the language expresses meteorological conditions (e.g., "it rains", "winds blow", "the sky freezes"). Many conlangs use zero-subject verbs or construct weather as active agents (e.g., "the sky throws water").
2. **Habitual Aspect**: Formulate grammatical markers for actions that occur regularly in nature or seasons (e.g., "bears hibernate in winter", "we harvest when the rain ends").
3. **Potentative Mood**: Establish syntax indicating the physical capability to survive or act in difficult conditions (e.g., "can walk through snow", "cannot breath smoke").
4. **Instrumental Case (Tools)**: Create markers for tools or materials used to survive (e.g., "cut *with an axe*", "build *with pine branches*").

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 9.1 Finding Water
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: The water skins are empty. We must find a spring soon.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: Look for the green willow trees down in the gulch. They only grow where water is close to the surface.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Let's climb down. Watch out for loose stones.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: Is this water safe to drink?
- **Speaker B (Forager)**: No, it is stagnant. We must boil it first or filter it through sand and charcoal.

### 9.2 Foraging Edibles
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Are these purple roots edible?
- **Speaker B (Forager)**: No! Those are death-roots. They will stop your heart. Look for the broad leaves with the yellow veins instead.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Good thing I asked. Let's harvest the yellow-veined ones.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Forager)**: These berries are sour but nutritious.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Gather as many as you can pack. We will dry them by the fire.

### 9.3 Hunting & Tracking
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Hunter 1)**: Look here. The grass is bent and the clay is indented. Deer tracks.
- **Speaker B (Hunter 2)**: The mud is still wet. The herd passed through less than an hour ago.
- **Speaker A (Hunter 1)**: Move quietly. Keep downwind so they don't smell us.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Hunter)**: Aim for the heart. Do not shoot until the beast stops grazing.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Drawing my bow... now.

### 9.4 Building Emergency Shelter
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Survivor 1)**: The sun is setting, and the temperature is dropping. We need shelter.
- **Speaker B (Survivor 2)**: Help me drag these fallen branches against this rock face. We can cover them with pine boughs.
- **Speaker A (Survivor 1)**: I will harvest moss to seal the gaps against the wind.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Companion)**: Is the shelter sturdy enough for the storm?
- **Speaker B (Survivor)**: It will keep the snow off us. Lay close to share body heat.

### 9.5 Starting Campfires
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: The wood is damp. The sparks are dying before the tinder catches.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: Shave the bark off the inner pine branch. The resin inside is dry and will burn easily.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Give me the flint and steel. I will try again.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Blow gently on the glowing coals. Don't smother it.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: It's catching! Add the small twigs now.

### 9.6 Preparing for Storms
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: Look at the black clouds rolling over the peak. That is a blizzard.
- **Speaker B (Leader)**: Tie the pack animals securely under the overhang. Double the ropes on the tents.
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: I will stack the firewood inside so it stays dry.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: The wind is howling. Bar the barn doors.
- **Speaker B (Son)**: The cattle are inside, father. The shutters are locked.

### 9.7 Surviving Extremes
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: The heat is oppressive. The desert sand is burning through my boot soles.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: Cover your head with your linen scarf. Rest in the shadow of this boulder until the sun passes the meridian.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Sip the water slowly. Do not gulp it.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Mountaineer)**: I cannot feel my toes. The cold is biting.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Remove your boots. Rub your feet with dry wool near the hearth fire.

### 9.8 Farming & Harvesting
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: The wheat is golden. The kernels are hard.
- **Speaker B (Son)**: Should we start the reaping tomorrow?
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: Yes, before the autumn rain rots the crop. Gather the sickles.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: The soil is too dry to plow.
- **Speaker B (Foreman)**: We must open the irrigation channels to soften the earth first.

### 9.9 Describing Flora/Fauna
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: This tree has smooth white bark and leaves shaped like stars.
- **Speaker B (Local)**: That is the silver-leaf. Its sap can be boiled into a sweet glue.
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: And what about that large bird with the blue crest?
- **Speaker B (Local)**: It is a cloud-jay. It mimics the calls of predators to protect its nest.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Scholar)**: These tracks are wide, with no claw marks visible.
- **Speaker B (Hunter)**: A mountain lion. The claws retract when it walks.

### 9.10 Gathering Raw Materials
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Builder)**: We need straight cedar poles for the roof rafters.
- **Speaker B (Lumberjack)**: The cedar grove is a mile up the valley. I will mark the best trees for felling.
- **Speaker A (Builder)**: Excellent. We also require flat slate stones for the hearth.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Forager)**: Collect the clay from the riverbank.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Make sure it's free of sand, or the pots will crack in the kiln.
