# 22. Animals, Beast Taming & Familiars

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Animacy Noun Classes**: Many conlangs segregate nouns into Animate (rational/animal) and Inanimate (object) genders. Animals and familiars require specific verb agreement suffixes and pronoun forms.
2. **Direct Animal Vocative Imperatives**: Define a set of short, direct grammatical endings used exclusively for animal commands (e.g., "heel", "halt", "fetch").
3. **Veterinary Stative Construction**: For sick or injured beasts, outline how physical conditions are marked (e.g., "the horse exhibits hoof-lameness").
4. **Relational Postpositions for Gear**: Formulate postpositions indicating where saddles, harnesses, and tracking links are placed relative to the animal's body.

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 22.1 Purchasing Livestock
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: I am looking for a sturdy ox to pull the plow.
- **Speaker B (Seller)**: This red ox is four years old, healthy, and has a steady gait.
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: Let me check his teeth. Decent. What is the asking price?
- **Speaker B (Seller)**: Twelve silver coins, or equivalent in iron.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Buyer)**: Do you have egg-laying hens?
- **Speaker B (Seller)**: I have ten hens in the coop. One copper piece for two hens.

### 22.2 Taming Predators
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Tamer)**: Do not look the wolf in the eyes. It is a sign of challenge.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: Should I offer the raw meat now?
- **Speaker A (Tamer)**: Keep your hand flat and low. Let him approach and sniff the scent first.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Companion)**: The shadow-panther is growling.
- **Speaker B (Tamer)**: Stand still. Do not run. Retreat slowly without turning your back.

### 22.3 Stable Mount Care
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Stable Hand)**: Brush the mud off the horse's hocks before you stable him.
- **Speaker B (Rider)**: I will. Clean the hooves too; he was limping on the gravel path.
- **Speaker A (Stable Hand)**: I will apply the pine-tar ointment to the frog of the hoof.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: Give the mare extra oats tonight. She carried me forty miles.
- **Speaker B (Stable Hand)**: Already done, sir. Fresh straw is in her stall.

### 22.4 Falconry & Hunting Beast Commands
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Falconer)**: Unhood the hawk. She has spotted the rabbit in the clearing.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Casting off now... Fly!
- **Speaker A (Falconer)**: Call her back with the whistle. She has secured the prey.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Hunter)**: Send the hound into the brush to flush the pheasant.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Go, boy! Fetch!

### 22.5 Veterinary Emergencies
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: My mount is breathing heavily and refusal to drink water.
- **Speaker B (Stable Master)**: The horse has colic. We must keep him walking. Do not let him lie down.
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: I will lead him around the paddock.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Farmer)**: The cow is having difficulty calving.
- **Speaker B (Vet)**: The calf is positioned backward. I must reach in and turn it.

### 22.6 Describing Wild Animal Behaviors
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: The black bears are nesting early this season.
- **Speaker B (Commander)**: That indicates an early and severe winter.
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: Yes, they are feeding heavily on the mountain berries.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Why are the birds flying in circles over the ridge?
- **Speaker B (Guide)**: There is a carcass below. A predator is feeding.

### 22.7 Calming a Panicked Mount
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: Woah... steady girl. It is only the thunder. Calm down.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Pat her neck and cover her eyes with your scarf.
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: Easy now... she is stopping her tremors.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: The mule is refusing to cross the wooden bridge.
- **Speaker B (Driver)**: Do not whip him. Lead him by the bridle and talk softly.

### 22.8 Training Commands
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Tamer)**: Sit! ... Stay! ... Good boy. Receive this meat treat.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: He responds well to the hand signs.
- **Speaker A (Tamer)**: Consistency is key. Never change the tone of your command.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Owner)**: Guard the camp.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: The hound is sitting by the fire, ears alert.

### 22.9 Pack Logistics
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Caravan Boss)**: The left basket on the mule is too heavy. The harness is rubbing her shoulder raw.
- **Speaker B (Worker)**: I will shift three iron bars to the right basket to balance the weight.
- **Speaker A (Caravaneers)**: Add some straw under the strap to protect the skin.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Guide)**: The donkey cannot carry more than eighty pounds of cargo.
- **Speaker B (Merchant)**: Take the tent poles off his pack and add them to the wagon.

### 22.10 Familiar/Spirit Bonding
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Mage)**: Close your eyes and focus on your breath. Can you feel the heartbeat of the fire-salamander?
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: Yes, it feels like a warm pulse in my palm.
- **Speaker A (Mage)**: The bond is established. The spirit will share its sight with you.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Sorcerer)**: Speak to your crow familiar. What does it see from the canopy?
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: It reports two riders approaching the gate.
