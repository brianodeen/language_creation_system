# 13. Craft, Guilds & Apprenticeship

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Instructional Imperatives**: Craft instructions require precise step-by-step commands (e.g., "Heat the iron", "strike hard", "quench immediately").
2. **Progressive Aspect**: Define how the language expresses ongoing processes of creation or learning (e.g., "I am copying the text", "he is learning the trade").
3. **Instrumental & Locative Nominalization**: Create suffixes that derive tools or workplaces from action verbs (e.g., *to weave* $\rightarrow$ *weaving-tool/loom*, *to smelt* $\rightarrow$ *smelting-place/forge*).
4. **Causative-Permissive Verb Derivations**: Formulate affixes that mean "to let someone learn" or "to make someone work".

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 13.1 Blacksmithing
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Master)**: Pull the bellows! The iron must be white-hot before we place it on the anvil.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I am pumping the bellows now, Master. The coals are glowing.
- **Speaker A (Master)**: Good. Now, take the tongs, lift the ingot, and hold it steady.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Apprentice)**: The blade cracked when I quenched it in the oil water.
- **Speaker B (Master)**: You heated it too fast. The carbon distribution was uneven. Try again.

### 13.2 Carpentry & Masonry
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Mason)**: We need to square this stone block. The edges are uneven.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I will use the chisel and hammer to smooth the corners.
- **Speaker A (Mason)**: Measure twice. If the block is not square, the arch will collapse.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Carpenter)**: The mortise and tenon joint is too tight.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I will shave a millimeter off the peg with the drawknife.

### 13.3 Tailoring & Leatherwork
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Master Tailor)**: This silk is delicate. Use the fine bone needle and thin thread.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: Should I double-stitch the sleeve seam?
- **Speaker A (Master Tailor)**: Yes, but keep the tension loose so the fabric does not pucker.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Leatherworker)**: Punch five holes along the belt strap.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I am using the brass punch. Are they spaced evenly?

### 13.4 Scribes & Manuscripts
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Abbot)**: You must copy this page exactly. Do not alter any letterforms.
- **Speaker B (Scribe)**: My ink is running thin, Master.
- **Speaker A (Abbot)**: Grind more soot with the water. The script must remain deep black.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Novice)**: I made an error in the third line of the page.
- **Speaker B (Scribe)**: Scrape it off gently with the knife blade. Do not tear the parchment.

### 13.5 Guild Enrollment
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Apprentice Candidate)**: I wish to apply for entry into the weavers' guild.
- **Speaker B (Guild Master)**: You must present a letter from a sponsor and pay the three-copper entry fee.
- **Speaker A (Apprentice Candidate)**: Here is the letter from Master Eldon, and the coppers.
- **Speaker B (Guild Master)**: Your name is registered. Welcome to the guild registry.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Guild Inspector)**: Show me your guild medallion.
- **Speaker B (Merchant)**: Here it is. Imprinted with the hammer and forge crest.

### 13.6 Apprentice Rules
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Master)**: These are the rules of my forge: you arrive at dawn, you sweep the floor, and you do not touch the tools without permission.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I understand, Master.
- **Speaker A (Master)**: If you violate these rules, your apprenticeship is ended.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Apprentice)**: Can I visit my family during the festival?
- **Speaker B (Master)**: Only after the orders are finished. Work comes first.

### 13.7 Appraising Masterpieces
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Guild Master)**: This shield is your masterpiece submission. Let us inspect the joinery.
- **Speaker B (Journeyman)**: I spent three months reinforcing the brass rim.
- **Speaker A (Guild Master)**: Excellent work. The welds are flawless. You are promoted to Master.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Inspector)**: The glaze on these pots is bubbling. This work is rejected.
- **Speaker B (Potter)**: I will refire the kiln and adjust the clay mix.

### 13.8 Skills Instruction
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Instructor)**: Place the wool on the spindle. Spin it clockwise to twist the fibers.
- **Speaker B (Student)**: My thread is too thick and bumpy.
- **Speaker A (Instructor)**: You are pulling too fast. Keep your fingers steady and pull slowly.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Master)**: Watch how I carve the scrollwork on the chair leg.
- **Speaker B (Apprentice)**: I am observing your wrist angle.

### 13.9 Student Testing & Grading
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Examiner)**: You have two hours to translate this text into the formal register.
- **Speaker B (Student)**: May I consult the lexicon?
- **Speaker A (Examiner)**: No lexicons. Only your memory.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Student)**: What was my grade on the metallurgical exam?
- **Speaker B (Instructor)**: You passed, but you must study the copper-tin ratios again.

### 13.10 Guild Master & Merchant Disputes
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Merchant)**: The weavers' guild is charging me double the port fee for my silk crates!
- **Speaker B (Guild Master)**: Your crates exceeded the weight limit stated in the charter.
- **Speaker A (Merchant)**: Let us consult the public charter text.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Guild Inspector)**: You are selling leather armor without a guild license!
- **Speaker B (Unlicensed Smith)**: I only sell to the local militia. I am not trading in the market.
- **Speaker A (Guild Inspector)**: The law applies to all. Close the shop.
