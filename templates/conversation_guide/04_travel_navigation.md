# 4. Travel, Navigation & Geography

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Directional Cases**: Formulate suffix systems for motion towards (**Lative Case**), motion out of (**Elative Case**), motion from (**Ablative Case**), and motion through (**Perlative/Prosecutive Case**).
2. **Spatial Postpositions**: Create a clear grid of relational adverbs (e.g., "north of the river", "beyond the mountains", "adjacent to the gate").
3. **Distance Modifiers**: Define units of measurement and how they are grammaticized (e.g., day-marches, paces, leagues).
4. **Locative Focus Particles**: Suffixes or markers that emphasize the location of a landmark (e.g., "The city is *right there*").

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 4.1 Asking & Giving Directions
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Excuse me, how do we reach the ruins in the valley?
- **Speaker B (Local)**: Follow the river north until you see the stone bridge. Do not cross it; turn east onto the dirt track.
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Is the ruins path steep?
- **Speaker B (Local)**: Only at the final ridge. It is about a three-hour hike.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: Which road leads to the capital?
- **Speaker B (Merchant)**: Take the left fork at the ancient oak tree. It is the paved road.

### 4.2 Map Reading & Star Charts
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Navigator)**: According to the map, there should be a well near this junction.
- **Speaker B (Captain)**: The map is twenty years old. The well might be dried up or overgrown.
- **Speaker A (Navigator)**: True. Let's check the celestial alignment to confirm our coordinates.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Sailor)**: The fog is too thick to see the coastline.
- **Speaker B (Navigator)**: Use the compass. Maintain a course of twenty degrees east of the North Star.

### 4.3 Describing Landscapes
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: The terrain ahead is marshy and thick with scrub oak.
- **Speaker B (Commander)**: Can the heavy supply wagons pass through?
- **Speaker A (Scout)**: No, we must detour along the rocky ridge to the west.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Look at the snow-draped peaks ahead.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: Beautiful, but dangerous. The high passes will be blocked.

### 4.4 Hiring Guides & Scouts
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: We need a guide who knows the paths through the volcanic wastes.
- **Speaker B (Local)**: Old Kaelen is the only one who survives out there. He knows the sulfur vents.
- **Speaker A (Explorer)**: Where can we find him?
- **Speaker B (Local)**: He is usually at the tavern near the north wall.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Merchant)**: Will you scout the forest road ahead of my caravan?
- **Speaker B (Scout)**: For three silver coins, I will check the canopy and clear the paths.

### 4.5 Mount & Draft Animal Care
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Stable Hand)**: Your horse threw a shoe on the stony trail.
- **Speaker B (Rider)**: Can you replace it before morning? We must leave early.
- **Speaker A (Stable Hand)**: Yes, I will have the farrier check the hoof immediately.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Caravaneer)**: The pack mules are exhausted. We must let them rest.
- **Speaker B (Merchant)**: Loosen their packs and lead them to the watering trough.

### 4.6 Road Hazards & Weather
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: The rain has turned the road into a mud slide.
- **Speaker B (Driver)**: The wagon wheels are sinking to the axles. We are stuck.
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Cut some branches to place under the tires for traction.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Rider)**: Watch out! There is a rockfall blocking the narrow pass.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Turn back. We cannot climb over with the horses.

### 4.7 Lost in the Wilderness
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: We have passed this same crooked tree three times. We are walking in circles.
- **Speaker B (Traveler 2)**: The compass must be affected by the local magnetic rock.
- **Speaker A (Traveler 1)**: Stop. Let's wait until sunset so we can navigate by the stars.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Hiker)**: I cannot find the trail markers.
- **Speaker B (Partner)**: Look for the stacked stones. They indicate the path.

### 4.8 Border Checkpoints
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Gate Guard)**: Halt. State your name and business in the kingdom.
- **Speaker B (Traveler)**: I am Master Robert, a textile trader. I have my transit permits here.
- **Speaker A (Gate Guard)**: Show me your papers. Open the second chest for inspection.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Officer)**: Your visa has expired, traveler.
- **Speaker B (Traveler)**: I was delayed by the flood at the river crossing. Can I renew it here?

### 4.9 Shipboard Commands
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Captain)**: Trim the sails! The wind is shifting north.
- **Speaker B (First Mate)**: Yes, Captain. Adjusting the main sheet now.
- **Speaker A (Captain)**: Watch the reef to the starboard side.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Sailor)**: The hold is taking on water, sir!
- **Speaker B (Officer)**: Man the bilge pumps! Find the leak and plug it.

### 4.10 Entering Ports & Cities
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Pilot)**: Harbor master, requesting permission to dock at slip four.
- **Speaker B (Harbor Master)**: Docking granted. Drop anchor and wait for the quarantine officer.
- **Speaker A (Pilot)**: Understood. We are throwing the lines now.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Visitor)**: What is the fee to enter the city gates?
- **Speaker B (Guard)**: One copper coin per person. Weapons must be peace-bound.
