# 18. Emotions, Comfort & Relationships

## Grammatical Mapping & Analysis
When translating this category into a target language, conlangers and linguists should establish the following grammatical features:
1. **Stative Verbs**: Define if emotions are expressed as adjectives, active verbs, or stative verbs (e.g., "to be angry" vs. "to anger-actively").
2. **Subjective Pronominal Clitics**: Create clitic pronouns that attach directly to emotional verbs to show the speaker's internal state (e.g., "I-sadly-walk", "she-joyfully-greets").
3. **Degree Modifiers**: Establish standard prefixes or suffixes that indicate the intensity of feelings (e.g., *a little*, *extremely*, *overbearingly*).
4. **Aspectual Emotional Transitions**: Differentiate between entering an emotional state (inchoative aspect: "to become sad") and ongoing states (imperfective: "to feel sad").

## Subcategories & Dialogues

### 18.1 Comforting Grief
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Comforter)**: I am so sorry for your loss. My heart breaks with yours.
- **Speaker B (Mourner)**: Thank you. The house feels empty without his laughter.
- **Speaker A (Comforter)**: Sit by the fire. You do not have to carry this grief alone.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Friend)**: Do not cry, young one. Your mother will return safely.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: I'm just so worried the storm will block the pass.

### 18.2 Sincere Friendships
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Friend 1)**: I appreciate your help with the harvest. I could not have finished it alone.
- **Speaker B (Friend 2)**: That is what friends are for, Robert. You helped me build the barn last year.
- **Speaker A (Friend 1)**: Truly. You are a brother to me.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Companion)**: I trust you with my life.
- **Speaker B (Partner)**: And I trust yours. Let us walk this path together.

### 18.3 Resolving Personal Disputes
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Colleague 1)**: I was angry when you spoke against my design in public.
- **Speaker B (Colleague 2)**: I apologize. I was focused on the structural safety, not your feelings. I should have spoken to you in private first.
- **Speaker A (Colleague 1)**: Thank you. Let us align our notes next time.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Neighbor)**: Your dog dug up my flower bed.
- **Speaker B (Owner)**: I am sorry. I will rebuild the fence today and replace the plants.

### 18.4 Sharing Deep Secrets
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Friend 1)**: I need to tell you something, but you must promise not to tell the village council.
- **Speaker B (Friend 2)**: My lips are sealed. What is the matter?
- **Speaker A (Friend 1)**: I found an ancient key hidden in the old well. I think it fits the temple gate.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Agent)**: The Duke is not who he claims to be.
- **Speaker B (Contact)**: Speak quietly. The walls have ears.

### 18.5 Romantic Courtship
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Suitor)**: I have watched you weave at the loom for months. Your focus is beautiful.
- **Speaker B (Weaver)**: You are sweet to say so, Alan. Your woodcarvings are very fine, too.
- **Speaker A (Suitor)**: Will you walk by the river with me this evening?

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Lover)**: I love you more than the stars.
- **Speaker B (Partner)**: And I love you. Let us build our home together.

### 18.6 Confronting Betrayal
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Partner 1)**: You told the guild master I was selling without a license! I trusted you!
- **Speaker B (Partner 2)**: They threatened to banish my family if I did not name the smuggler. I had no choice.
- **Speaker A (Partner 1)**: You always find a choice when it is your neck on the block. Leave this house.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Soldier)**: You took the gold to show the enemy the hidden tunnel.
- **Speaker B (Traitor)**: The gold was meant to buy my freedom!

### 18.7 Expressing Gratitude
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Recipient)**: Thank you from the bottom of my heart. This bread will keep my children alive.
- **Speaker B (Giver)**: You do not need to thank me. We are neighbors. We look out for one another.
- **Speaker A (Recipient)**: I will repay your kindness as soon as the harvest is sold.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Traveler)**: Thank you for pointing out the danger. The pass looked clear.
- **Speaker B (Local)**: The mountain is a quiet hunter. Be careful.

### 18.8 Expressing Frustration
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: I have been working on this joint for three hours, and it still does not fit!
- **Speaker B (Foreman)**: Take a deep breath. Step away from the bench for a few minutes. You are forcing the wood.
- **Speaker A (Worker)**: I'm just so tired of starting over.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Driver)**: The cart has broken down again! I hate this old road.
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: Screaming at the wheels will not fix the axle.

### 18.9 Soothing a Child
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Parent)**: Shh, little one. The thunder is only the sky cleaning its halls. You are safe.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: It's too loud, father.
- **Speaker A (Parent)**: Wrap this warm blanket tight. I will sing you the river lullaby.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Mother)**: Blow on your scraped knee. The pain will fly away.
- **Speaker B (Child)**: It hurts... but it's getting better.

### 18.10 Rebuilding Trust
#### Conversation 1
- **Speaker A (Friend 1)**: I know I let you down when I did not show up at the defense gates.
- **Speaker B (Friend 2)**: It cost us the outer storage barn, Julian. I had to defend the gate alone.
- **Speaker A (Friend 1)**: I know. Let me help you rebuild the barn. I will work for free.
- **Speaker B (Friend 2)**: Grab the hammer. Let's see if your hammer is stronger than your word.

#### Conversation 2
- **Speaker A (Partner)**: Can you ever forgive me?
- **Speaker B (Companion)**: It will take time, but we can start by cleaning the workshop together.
